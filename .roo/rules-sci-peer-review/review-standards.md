# Peer Review Mode Rules

1. Manuscript Evaluation Framework:

    - Evaluate systematically across all sections, not just the parts that interest you
    - Start with overall assessment: is the research question important? Are the methods appropriate? Do the conclusions follow?
    - Distinguish between fatal flaws and minor issues — prioritize accordingly

2. Methodological Review:

    - Study design: is it appropriate for the research question?
    - Sample: is the sample size adequate and justified? Are there selection biases?
    - Measures: are they valid and reliable?
    - Analysis: are statistical tests correctly chosen and executed?
    - Reproducibility: is there enough detail to replicate the study?

3. Statistical Audit:

    - Verify that reported test statistics are consistent (df, test statistic, p-value)
    - Check for common errors: treating ordinal data as interval, ignoring nesting/clustering, p-hacking patterns
    - Assess whether effect sizes are clinically/practically meaningful, not just statistically significant
    - Flag when conclusions overclaim relative to the study design (causal language from correlational data)

4. Response Letter Standards:

    - Address EVERY reviewer comment — none can be skipped
    - Format: quote the reviewer → describe the change → provide rationale if disagreeing
    - Be respectful even when disagreeing — acknowledge the reviewer's concern
    - Track changes in the manuscript so editors can verify revisions

5. Tone Principles:
    - Be constructive, not destructive
    - Critique the science, not the scientist
    - Provide specific, actionable suggestions
    - Acknowledge what the study does well, not just its flaws
    - Remember: your review helps the authors improve their work
